# Mission Forge — suggested judge demonstration

## Core statement

Build a spacecraft, live with your engineering decisions, and bring scientific evidence home.

## Suggested three-minute walkthrough

This is a presentation suggestion, not an event pitch rule.

1. **0:00–0:25 — The problem:** mission design involves coupled trade-offs. This game makes them understandable through decisions and consequences.
2. **0:25–0:55 — Assembly:** install power and propulsion modules. Show delta-v and power margin. An ion / radioisotope mismatch blocks launch. The assembled ship continues into flight.
3. **0:55–1:20 — Planning:** compare reserve-first and rapid routes. Identify schedules as fictional and resource estimates as game reserves.
4. **1:20–1:45 — Operations:** demonstrate power-bus isolation and the slower-scanning compromise.
5. **1:45–2:15 — Science:** acquire two training bands and compare landing sites. Show the NASA reference and synthetic-data label.
6. **2:15–2:40 — Evidence:** explain why minerals support water-related alteration but cannot establish life. Show the decision debrief.
7. **2:40–3:00 — Impact:** a keyboard-accessible learning game for engineering and scientific reasoning. The next data step is integrating the released challenge pack with preserved source, units and uncertainty.

Use clearly labelled recorded gameplay for later chapters if live time is too short. Do not present a recording as a live playthrough.

## Current scientific scope

The game uses ideal rocket-equation delta-v, thrust/mass acceleration and inverse-square solar power. Flight time, resource percentages, routes, hardware ratings and mineral readings are authored educational scenarios. This is not a trajectory planner. The linked NASA Curiosity findings inform the interpretation of mineral evidence; no measured spectral dataset is currently imported.
